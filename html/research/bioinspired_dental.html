Bio-Inspired Functionally Graded Materials in Dental Composites

On average, adults develop three to four cavities throughout their lifetime, with some individuals experiencing more than ten cavities [2]. In case of minor damage to the tooth structure, dentists typically use fillings to restore the tooth's function and aesthetics. However, in case of extensive damage, a dental crown may be necessary to provide additional support and protection to the remaining tooth structure [3]. Dental crowns are custom-made caps that cover the entire tooth, providing strength and durability. They are typically made from materials such as porcelain for aesthetic purposes and ceramic for strength [4].

The typical lifespan of a all-ceramic dental crown is around 10 to 15 years [5]. Furthermore, an average dental crown can cost between $800 and $2,500 [6]. This makes it imperative that we develop more durable and cost-effective dental materials to improve the longevity of dental restorations and reduce the financial burden on patients.

The most common site of failure for dental crowns is the interface between the crown and the tooth structure, which can lead to debonding, microleakage, and secondary caries [6]. This is due to the significant difference in mechanical properties between the crown material and the natural tooth structure which is bonded using an adhesive layer causing a significant mismatch in mechanical properties at the interface, leading to stress concentrations and eventual failure [7].

One promising approach to achieve this is through the use of bio-inspired functionally graded materials (FGMs) in all-ceramic restorations. The dentinoenamel junction (DEJ) is a natural interface between the enamel and dentin layers of a tooth, which exhibits a gradual transition in mechanical properties [1]. This gradient in properties helps to distribute stresses and prevent failure at the interface, making it an ideal model for mimicking nature tooth structure in dental restorations.

To mimic the DEJ, this research develops a finite element model to study the behavior of FGMs in Abaqus to investigate the effect of different gradation profiles on the stress distribution and failure behavior of crowns. The study considers various gradation profiles, such as linear, exponential, sigmoidal, parabolic, and logarithmic, to determine which profile provides the best performance in terms of reducing stress concentrations and improving durability. The results of this study can provide insights into the design of bio-inspired crowns that better mimic the natural tooth structure and improve the longevity of dental restorations.

The broader implications of this research extend beyond dental applications. The principles of bio-inspired design and FGMs can be applied to a wide range of engineering problems, including aerospace, automotive, and biomedical applications. By learning from nature's designs, we can develop innovative materials and structures that are more efficient, durable, and sustainable.
